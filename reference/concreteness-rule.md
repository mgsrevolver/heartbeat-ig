## 13. Marketing copy: the concreteness rule

*Codified 2026-08-26 from Nick's review of Resources launch copy. Applies to every headline, subject line, preview line, CTA, popup/tour card, ad line and social one-liner. It is the highest-frequency failure in AI-drafted marketing copy, so check it first.*

**The rule: name the noun and the number. If a word could be swapped for a different product's word without changing the sentence, the sentence is not doing any work.**

Vague copy fails in three distinct ways, & the third one is the dangerous one.

### Failure 1: the placeholder noun

Words like *something*, *value*, *results*, *more*, *what you've made*, *the thing* are stand-ins for a noun the writer didn't pick. The reader has to do the work of guessing, & a reader guessing is a reader not converting.

> ❌ **Every reader leaves you something**
> ✅ **You keep their email address even when they don't buy**

*Nick's note on the bad version:* "what is something? sounds ambiguous & doesn't get me excited."

### Failure 2: the undefined actor

*Reader, user, people, they, someone* — if the audience can't tell whether that person is a stranger, a member, or a buyer, they can't tell whether the line is an opportunity or a chore.

> ❌ **Every reader, in a list you own**
> ✅ **Every email address your guide collected, in one place**

*Nick's note:* "who's the reader? doesn't sound like an opportunity to me."

### Failure 3: vagueness that inverts the meaning

This is the one to watch. A hedged, abstract phrasing can carry a connotation opposite to the one intended, & the writer won't notice bc they know what they meant.

> ❌ **Your best resource can have a price**
> ✅ **Sell your onboarding guide without selling a membership**

*Nick's note:* "'have a price' is the most awkward way to say something can be paid, bc it also has a negative connotation. For something to have a price is to have consequence. So by being vague you've turned a positive headline into a negative one."

Same trap: *"put a price on it"* (sounds like a cost), *"what this will cost you"*, *"the price of growth"*. Read every headline once asking **"could this be read as a warning?"**

### The swap table

| ❌ Vague | ✅ Concrete |
|---|---|
| "Every reader leaves you something" | "You keep their email address even when they don't buy" |
| "Your best resource can have a price" | "Sell your onboarding guide without selling a membership" |
| "Sell what you've already made" | "Sell the workbook you already wrote" |
| "You have more leads than you think" | "Every email address you've collected since 2024, in one list" |
| "Put a price on it" | "Charge $29 for it" |
| "Stop asking strangers to leap" | "Stop asking strangers for $49 a month" |
| "Drive more value from your content" | "Charge for the replay library you already recorded" |
| "See your leads" | "See who's already on your list" |
| "Unlock new revenue" | "Take a $29 payment from someone who isn't a member" |
| "Pick your best resource" | "Pick the guide you always end up sending people" |

### The test, in order

1. **Circle every noun.** Any of them a placeholder? Replace it with the literal object: an email address, a PDF, $29, the onboarding guide.
2. **Name the person.** Stranger, member, buyer, someone who found you on Instagram. Not "the user."
3. **Check the verb.** *Can have, helps you, allows you to, enables* are all hedges. Use the verb for the thing that actually happens: sell, charge, keep, collect, publish.
4. **Read it as a warning.** If the line survives a hostile reading, it's clean. If "have a price" reads as "there will be consequences," rewrite.
5. **Swap test.** Could this exact line sit on Circle's homepage? If yes, it says nothing about Heartbeat & nothing about the product.

**Where a specific example beats a general one:** a broad audience is *more* likely to see themselves in "the workbook you already wrote" than in "what you've already made." The specific noun invites substitution ("oh, my replay library"). The general noun invites skimming. Always name one concrete artifact & let the reader swap in theirs.
